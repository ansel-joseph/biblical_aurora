PARABLE OF JESUS

The Lost Sheep

Matthew 18:12–14 · Luke 15:3–7

A shepherd leaves the ninety-nine sheep behind to search for the one that has wandered away.

The Story

Jesus tells of a shepherd who has one hundred sheep. When one of them wanders away, the shepherd leaves the ninety-nine in the open country and goes searching for the lost one.

When he finds it, he rejoices and carries it home. Jesus then explains that there is great joy over one sinner who repents.

SCRIPTURE

Matthew 18:12–14

"What do you think? If a man owns a hundred sheep, and one of them wanders away, will he not leave the ninety-nine on the hills and go to look for the one that wandered off?"

Matthew 18:12

Historical & Cultural Context

Shepherding was a familiar part of life in the world of Jesus. Sheep depended heavily upon their shepherd for protection, guidance, and finding safe pasture.

A sheep wandering away from the flock could therefore face serious danger. Jesus uses this familiar image to communicate something much deeper about God's concern for the individual.

THE MEANING

Why does Jesus focus on the one?

The parable reveals the extraordinary value God places upon the individual. The one who has wandered away is not forgotten simply because ninety-nine remain.

Jesus presents God as one who actively seeks the lost and rejoices when they are restored.

Key Themes

Mercy

God does not abandon those who have wandered.

Repentance

Returning to God is met with joy rather than rejection.

Human Worth

Every individual possesses immense value before God.

Questions for Reflection

  1. What does the shepherd's search reveal about God's character?
  2. Why do you think Jesus emphasizes the one lost sheep?
  3. How does this parable challenge the way we view people who have wandered from faith?
